# Business mail dataset — implementation plan

> Living plan. Each step is done test-first (CLAUDE.md §0) where it produces code, and updates the README in the same
> change. The prompts the generator uses are in `business-dataset-prompts.md`; the master prompt for implementation
> sessions is at the top of that file.
> **Status:** slice 1 (6–31 July 2026, 305 messages) built, validated and label-audited; eval steps 1–5 implemented. Open: human review (Q) of slice 1, a first replay run (needs Docker), slices 2–3. See *Implementation notes* at the end.

## Goal

A dense, labelled, **fully fictional** dataset of business email — one company's mailbox over twelve weeks — that the
AI evaluation can replay: orders coming in and going out, shipments across Europe in both directions, meetings, to-dos,
invoices, frustrated and leaving customers, new customers, smooth operations, noise, and the edge cases that break naive
extraction (several carriers sharing one driver, one topic in several threads, several topics in one thread, mails
nobody answers). It serves every evaluation contor has and the one it is missing:

| Evaluation | What the dataset adds |
|---|---|
| Extraction (`AiExtractionEvalTest`) | business collections (customers, suppliers, sales/purchase orders, shipments, complaints) on top of the personal ones; hundreds of labelled mails instead of 49 |
| Reply triage + drafts (`AiReplyTriageEvalTest`, `AiReplyDraftEvalTest`) | `needsReply`, intent, questions, scheduling windows, courtesy kinds on real business mail |
| Multi-mail context (`AiContextEvalTest`) | threads and sender histories that *require* earlier mail (reminders without numbers, "as discussed", changed delivery dates) |
| Insights (`AiInsightEvalTest`) | storylines whose point only shows across mails (a customer ordering less each month, a carrier late three times, a promised callback that never came) |
| Importance | an expected importance band per proposal-worthy mail |
| **new: mailbox replay** | the whole mailbox processed in time order by the real pipeline (worker, dedupe, context, proposals) against expected *end states* of every collection |

Out of scope: real data of any kind, attachments as files (mentioned in text only), HTML layout testing.

## Decisions (proposed defaults — confirm or change)

1. **One fictional company, one mailbox owner, twelve weeks.** The world: *Nordhafen Industriebedarf GmbH*, a
   Hamburg wholesaler of industrial tools and spare parts selling B2B across Europe and buying from suppliers in
   Poland, Spain, Italy, Czechia and Turkey. Mailbox owner: Alex Morgan, Head of Operations, plus a shared
   `orders@` mailbox (two accounts → unified inbox edge cases). Period: Monday 2026-07-06 to Friday 2026-09-25.
2. **Fictional and safe by construction.** Only RFC 2606 domains (`*.example`), invented company and person names
   checked against a deny list of real brands, phone numbers in an obviously fictional pattern (`+49 40 5550 xxxx`),
   no IBANs or card numbers (payment details only as "account on file"), no real people's names of public figures.
3. **Labels first, text second.** A storyline is written as structured beats with the facts and labels; the email
   text is rendered from a beat afterwards. Labels are therefore never reverse-engineered from prose, and every labelled
   value can be checked to occur in (or be derivable from) the text.
4. **Generated by a different model than the one under test.** Claude generates and checks; the local `qwen3.5:9b`
   is only ever evaluated on it (no self-grading bias). Every step is reproducible from committed inputs (world bible,
   storylines, seeds) and checked by deterministic validators.
5. **Size: ~1 200 messages in ~260 threads**, about 70 % inbound, 30 % sent by the user (Sent Items), ~20 % noise
   (see *Volume and mix*). Dense: most business mails carry 2–4 labelled records; many mails depend on earlier ones.
6. **Business eval schema in addition to the personal one.** `EvalCollections.business()` adds customers, suppliers,
   sales_orders, purchase_orders, shipments, complaints (field lists below). The system collections (people,
   organizations, events, tasks) stay as they are. The existing synthetic corpus and baseline are untouched.
7. **Languages: 60 % German, 35 % English, 5 % mixed/other** (Polish, Spanish or Italian senders writing short English
   or German, a few with a native-language greeting or signature).
8. **Committed, not private.** The dataset is fictional, so it lives in the repo (`ai-eval/business/`) and runs in CI-free
   `-Pai-eval` like the rest.

## The world (world bible)

Generated once (prompt W in `business-dataset-prompts.md`), reviewed by a human, then frozen as `world.json`. Every
storyline may only use entities from it. Minimum cast:

| Group | Count | Notes / edge cases they enable |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | 6 | Alex (owner of the mailbox), warehouse lead, accounting, sales rep DACH, sales rep export, managing director. Internal mail: forwards, "can you handle this?", CC chains |
| Customers | 22 | segments: 6 **loyal** (steady orders), 4 **growing**, 4 **at risk** (shrinking orders, complaints, competitor mentioned), 2 **lost** (goodbye mail or silence), 3 **new** (onboarding, first order, credit check), 3 **one-off**. Countries DE, AT, CH (non-EU: customs), NL, DK, FR, PL, SE, NO (non-EU) |
| Suppliers | 9 | PL (castings), ES (fasteners), IT (precision tools), CZ (packaging), TR (forgings — the "across the continent" lane, 4–6 days road freight + Bulgarian/Turkish border), DE domestic ×3, one supplier that raises prices mid-period |
| Carriers / logistics | 7 | road freight PL, road freight ES/PT, Alpine groupage, parcel service, pallet network, Turkey–EU trucking, a freight forwarder that subcontracts. **Two carriers share one subcontracted driver** (same name, same mobile, different employer signatures, once writing from a private address) |
| Service providers | 6 | bank (notifications only), insurer (transport damage claim), IT/ERP vendor, trade fair organiser, accountant firm, cleaning/facility |
| Noise senders | 10 | newsletters, webinars, ERP notifications, marketplace alerts, LinkedIn-style digests, phishing-looking mails (must produce nothing), automated "delivery attempted" spam |

Each entity: id, name, type, country, language, time zone, domain, people (name, role, email, phone, writing style:
formal/informal, Sie/du, verbose/terse, typo rate), relationships (customer of, supplier of, subcontractor of),
segment, and a **ledger** of numbers it owns (customer number, price list, payment terms, account manager).

Number formats are fixed per issuer so they can be matched: sales orders `SO-26-0xxx`, purchase orders `PO-26-1xxx`,
outbound invoices `RE-2026-0xxx`, supplier invoices in the supplier's own format, shipments by carrier format
(`BRF-PL-xxxxxx`, CMR numbers, parcel tracking), complaints `RMA-26-0xx`.

## Business eval schema (`EvalCollections.business()`)

On top of the system collections (`people`, `organizations`, `events`, `tasks`):

| Collection | Fields (type) | Match key |
|---|---|---|
| customers | name (TEXT, req), customer_number (TEXT), country (TEXT), segment (ENUM loyal/growing/at_risk/lost/new), payment_terms (TEXT), notes | customer_number, else name |
| suppliers | name (TEXT, req), country, lead_time_days (NUMBER), notes | name |
| sales_orders | order_number (TEXT, req), customer (TEXT), total (MONEY), state (STATUS received/confirmed/in_production/shipped/delivered/cancelled), requested_delivery (DATE), confirmed_delivery (DATE) | order_number |
| purchase_orders | po_number (TEXT, req), supplier (TEXT), total (MONEY), state (STATUS ordered/confirmed/shipped/received/cancelled), expected_arrival (DATE) | po_number |
| shipments | tracking_number (TEXT, req), carrier (TEXT), direction (ENUM outbound/inbound), order_ref (TEXT), state (STATUS booked/picked_up/in_transit/delayed/delivered/damaged), eta (DATE), driver (TEXT) | tracking_number |
| complaints | reference (TEXT, req), customer (TEXT), topic (ENUM delay/damage/wrong_item/quality/invoice/service), severity (ENUM low/medium/high), state (STATUS open/in_progress/resolved), opened_on (DATE) | reference |
| invoices (reused) | invoice_number, issuer, amount, due_date, state open/paid — now in both directions (`direction` added: incoming/outgoing) | invoice_number |

Relation types for the dataset: `works_at` (people → organizations), `attends` (people → events) as today, plus
`supplies` (suppliers → purchase_orders), `ships` (shipments → sales_orders / purchase_orders), `drives_for`
(people → organizations; the shared driver has two).

## Storyline catalogue

A storyline is a sequence of **beats** (one beat = one email or one sent reply) on a timeline, with the facts each beat
states and the labels it carries. Storylines interleave in the mailbox; several storylines share entities. The
catalogue below is the minimum; each entry gives the number of instances to generate and the edge cases to cover.

### A. Sales: orders coming in (≈ 32 storylines)
1. **RFQ → quote → order → confirmation** (8): customer asks for a quote (questions: price, lead time, MOQ), Alex
   quotes (sent), customer orders by mail with reference to the quote number only, order confirmation (sent).
   *Edge:* order mail repeats the quote number but not the prices → context needed; one customer orders a different
   quantity than quoted.
2. **Repeat order, terse** (8): "Same as last time, 40 boxes M8, delivery KW 32" — only resolvable with history.
3. **Rush order** (4): deadline within 48 h, importance URGENT, Saturday delivery request, partly impossible.
4. **Order change** (5): quantity/date changed after confirmation; second change contradicts the first; one
   reinstated after cancellation.
5. **Cancellation** (3): full and partial cancellation; one cancellation arriving after shipment (conflict).
6. **Order via shared `orders@` mailbox** (4): same order also CC'd to Alex → duplicate mail in two accounts.

### B. Outbound fulfilment and shipping (≈ 30)
1. **Dispatch notice + tracking** (10) from carriers and from the own warehouse; split shipments (one order, two
   tracking numbers); one shipment carrying two orders.
2. **Delay** (6): carrier announces delay; second delay; customer asks where the goods are (needs reply, questions).
3. **Damage / wrong item** (5): photos "attached", RMA opened, insurer involved, replacement shipped.
4. **Cross-border with customs** (CH, NO) (4): missing commercial invoice, goods held at customs, broker asks
   for EORI/HS code (to-do with deadline).
5. **Delivery confirmations, smooth** (5): proof of delivery, "all arrived, thanks!" — no reply needed or courtesy.

### C. Inbound procurement: shipping to us across the continent (≈ 24)
1. **PO → supplier confirmation → ASN → arrival** (9): Turkey (TIR, 5–6 days, border waiting times, CMR), Spain
   (FTL via France), Poland (groupage), Italy (express). Different time zones (TRT, CET), different date formats
   (`12.08.`, `08/12`, `12 Aug`) → date resolution edge cases.
2. **Partial delivery / backorder** (4): 600 of 1 000 pcs, rest "in two weeks" — PO stays open.
3. **Supplier price increase** (2): announcement, then invoices with the new price (insight: price change).
4. **Warehouse slot booking** (4): carrier asks for an unloading slot (scheduling, free times!), driver arrives early.
5. **Supplier quality issue** (2): incoming inspection fails, 8D report requested.
6. **Payment terms dispute** (3): supplier reminder, partial payment, skonto question.

### D. The shared driver (≈ 4 storylines, 14 mails) — entity resolution edge case
Marek (subcontractor) drives for the Polish road freight carrier *and* the pallet network. Mails: dispatcher of carrier
1 announces "driver Marek W., +48 …"; three weeks later carrier 2 announces "Fahrer Marek Wiśniewski, same number";
Marek writes himself from a private address ("stuck at the A2, arrive 14:30 instead of 11"); a mail names him with a
typo ("Marek Wisniewski"). **Expected:** one person record (same phone), `drives_for` both carriers, shipments of both
carriers with `driver` set; no duplicate person. A fifth mail mentions a *different* "Marek" (customer's buyer) —
must stay a separate person (different company, no phone match).

### E. Customer relations: frustrated, losing, winning (≈ 28)
1. **Escalation chain** (4): polite question → impatient follow-up → angry mail to managing director (CC Alex) →
   resolution or silence. Tone escalates; needsReply true each time; importance rises; complaint record state moves.
2. **Churn signals** (4 at-risk customers): order volumes shrinking month by month (only visible across mails →
   insight), mention of a competitor's offer, request for a "better price or we have to look elsewhere", one ends with
   a polite goodbye ("we have decided to source from another supplier") → courtesy reply (KEEP_IN_TOUCH or
   REJECTION_THANKS-like), customer segment → lost.
3. **Silent loss** (2): loyal customer's monthly order simply stops; last mail weeks ago (insight: customer went quiet).
4. **Win-back** (2): offer, meeting, new order.
5. **New customers** (5): first contact, credit check request to accounting, onboarding questions (needsReply),
   first order, thank-you (courtesy).
6. **Smooth operations** (11): "Delivery arrived complete, thank you", positive feedback, routine monthly order without
   issues, invoice paid on time notification → mostly no reply, some courtesy, low importance.

### F. Meetings and scheduling (≈ 16)
Customer visit requests with questions ("Tuesday or Wednesday next week?"), supplier audit, trade fair appointments
(day + hall + booth), internal weekly, reschedule, cancellation, a dinner invitation (evening slot), Teams links,
an invitation in English from a Turkish supplier with a time in TRT (time zone edge case), a meeting proposal where
Alex already answered (answered — no new draft).

### G. To-dos (embedded everywhere, plus ≈ 10 explicit)
Explicit requests with deadlines ("bitte bis Donnerstag die Zolltarifnummern schicken"), implied tasks (an invoice →
pay it; a customs hold → provide documents), internal delegations ("can you call them back?"), to-dos with relative
dates across weekends, to-dos that are later done (a follow-up mail says "received, thanks" → task state done),
to-dos in a forwarded mail (belongs to the forwarder's request, not the quoted mail).

### H. Finance (≈ 18)
Outgoing invoices (sent), payment received notifications, dunning levels 1–3 for a slow-paying customer, incoming
supplier invoices with skonto, a credit note, a currency case (CHF, SEK, TRY quoted, EUR invoiced), an invoice
dispute linked to a complaint.

### I. Noise and automation (≈ 230 mails)
Newsletters, webinar invitations, ERP job notifications, bank account statements notices, marketplace price alerts,
"your parcel could not be delivered" scam, LinkedIn-style digests, out-of-office replies (to Alex's sent mails),
bounces ("mailbox full"), read receipts, calendar auto-accepts. Expected: no records, no reply (except never), low.

### J. Pure edge cases (≈ 40 single mails, spread across storylines)
- Same subject, different topic ("Rechnung" from three issuers); different subjects, same topic (thread split by a
  customer who never replies in-thread).
- Several topics in one mail (an order + a complaint + a meeting request) → several records, one reply answering all.
- Reply-all chain where the quoted history contains the facts and the new text only says "ok, do it".
- Forwarded mail with the original sender's facts; Alex's comment on top contains the to-do.
- Mail with a question that needs no answer (rhetorical, FYI with "?") and an action without a question mark.
- Wrong recipient ("sorry, wrong Alex"), duplicate send (same mail twice, 2 minutes apart).
- Typos in order numbers (`SO-26-0142` vs `SO-26-142`), amounts with different separators (`1.234,50 €`, `€1,234.50`).
- Dates: relative ("nächsten Dienstag"), KW/calendar weeks ("KW 34"), US-style dates from a UK customer, a date that
  falls on a public holiday, "end of month", a time in another time zone.
- A sender who changes jobs (same person, new company signature) → person updated, works_at changes.
- Phishing-like mail imitating a carrier (must produce nothing, must not be replied to).
- Very long mail (terms and conditions pasted), very short mail ("Passt."), mail that is only a signature + "see attached".
- Internal mail in which Alex is only CC → no reply expected from Alex.

## File layout and format

```
contor-api/src/test/resources/ai-eval/business/
  world.json                    # frozen world bible (entities, people, ledgers, number formats)
  storylines/<id>.json          # one storyline: beats with facts + labels (the source of truth)
  mailbox/<message-id>.json     # one rendered message (generated from a beat), labels included
  manifest.json                 # generator version, seeds, counts per category, checksums
  expected/end-state.json       # expected content of every collection after the whole mailbox (replay eval)
  expected/insights.json        # expected insights: storyline, evidence message ids, as-of date
```

**Message file** (superset of `EvalCase`, so the existing loader reads it unchanged):

```json
{
  "id": "m-0417",
  "storyline": "E2-at-risk-kessler",
  "beat": 3,
  "account": "alex",                    // alex | orders (shared mailbox)
  "folder": "inbox",                    // inbox | sent
  "conversationId": "c-kessler-price",  // Outlook-style conversation
  "inReplyTo": "m-0388",
  "email": { "mailbox": "alex.morgan@nordhafen.example", "fromName": "…", "fromAddress": "…", "to": ["…"], "cc": ["…"],
             "subject": "…", "receivedAt": "2026-08-18T07:42:00Z", "body": "…" },
  "expected": { "records": { "<collection>": [ { "title": "…", "values": { … } } ] }, "relations": [ … ] },
  "mustNotProduce": [ … ],
  "needsReply": true,
  "reply": { "intent": "SCHEDULE", "questions": ["…"], "scheduleFrom": "2026-08-24", "scheduleTo": "2026-08-28",
             "timeOfDay": "ANY", "courtesyKind": null, "answeredBy": "m-0421" },
  "importance": "HIGH",                 // expected band of the main proposal
  "dependsOn": ["m-0388", "m-0390"],    // earlier messages needed to label this one fully (context eval)
  "tags": ["churn-signal", "competitor-mentioned"]
}
```

**Storyline file:** id, category, entities, summary, and `beats[]` with `day`, `time`, `direction`, `from`, `to`,
`facts` (exact numbers, dates, amounts the text must state), `labels` (as above), `style` (language, tone, length,
quoting, typos), `editorNotes` (what makes it hard). The renderer may only state the `facts`, may phrase them freely
and must not add new facts.

## Generation pipeline

| Phase | Input → output | How | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| W. World bible | decisions above → `world.json` | prompt W (Claude), one pass, human review | schema; only `.example` domains; deny-list of real brands; every customer/supplier has ≥ 1 person; segments add up |
| S. Storylines | world + catalogue entry + seed → `storylines/*.json` | prompt S per catalogue entry (batches of ≤ 5 storylines, so the ledger stays consistent) | schema; beats in time order; numbers from the ledger; labels complete; required edge cases of the entry present |
| T. Timeline merge | all storylines → global message ids, conversation ids, dates | deterministic script (seeded): assigns ids, spreads times by weekday rhythm, inserts noise and out-of-office mails | no message before its `inReplyTo`; weekend rule; holiday weeks |
| R. Rendering | beat + facts + style → message body | prompt R per beat (Claude), with the previous messages of the conversation for quoting | **fact check**: every labelled value occurs in the body (normalized: amounts, dates, numbers) or is marked `derivable` with the rule (relative date, calendar week, context); no extra numbers that look like order/tracking numbers; length within style bounds; language detected = requested |
| L. Label audit | message + labels → verdict | prompt L (Claude, separate call, no access to the storyline notes): "label this mail from scratch"; disagreements listed | agreement rate per label type; disagreements fixed by hand or re-rendered |
| E. Expected states | storylines → `expected/end-state.json`, `expected/insights.json` | deterministic fold over beats (last state wins, as of 2026-09-25 17:00) | every record in end state traceable to beats |
| Q. Human review | 10 % sample stratified by category + every edge case J | checklist (below) | sign-off recorded in `manifest.json` |

All generator calls store prompt version, model, seed and the exact input in `manifest.json`, so a message can be
re-rendered alone. Rendering is idempotent per message id.

### Human review checklist
Reads like real business mail of that sender (tone, length, signature) · facts match the labels exactly · no fact the
labels do not know · dates and weekdays consistent · the edge case it should exercise is actually there · nothing real
(names, brands, numbers) · German and English idiomatic (not translated-sounding) · quoting/forwarding looks like
Outlook.

## Labelling guidelines (binding for prompts S, R and L)

- **Records:** only facts stated in the mail or resolvable from `dependsOn` messages; title conventions per
  collection (sales order: `"<customer> <order_number>"`, shipment: `"<carrier> <tracking>"`, complaint:
  `"<reference> <customer> <topic>"`, person: full name, task: imperative verb first, event: `"<what> <with whom>"`).
- **Updates vs new:** a later mail about an existing order labels the *changed values only* under the same title
  (the replay eval checks the end state; the per-mail eval treats it as an expected record with those values).
- **Dates:** resolve relative dates against the mail's own date in the sender's time zone; calendar weeks → Monday
  of the week for `requested_delivery`, and `notes` keep "KW 34"; times as ISO with offset of the sender's zone.
- **needsReply:** true only if the sender waits for a written answer from Alex (same rule as the triage prompt:
  requests to *do* something are not replies). CC-only mails: false. Internal "can you…?" to Alex: true.
- **intent / questions:** the questions as the sender asked them (≤ 5); `answeredBy` points at Alex's sent reply
  when one exists in the mailbox (that mail must then not get a new draft — `answered`).
- **courtesyKind:** only personal mails that need no answer (goodbye of a customer, thank-you after smooth delivery,
  congratulations); never automated senders.
- **importance band:** URGENT (deadline ≤ 2 days with business impact, angry escalation, customs hold), HIGH (customer
  question about a delay, order change, payment dunning level 2+), NORMAL (orders, meetings), LOW (smooth confirmations,
  courtesy, most findings).
- **mustNotProduce:** for noise and phishing (any record), for CC-only internal mails (tasks), for the "different
  Marek" (a merge into the driver), for quoted history (records from the quote when the new text only says "ok").
- **Insights (expected/insights.json):** storyline, the evidence messages, and the earliest date from which the insight
  is fair to expect (e.g. the third shrinking order).

## Evaluation integration (code, test-first)

### Step 1 — Schema and loader (S)
- `EvalCollections.business()` with the collections and relation types above; `EvalCollectionsTest` extended.
- `BusinessCorpusTest` (like `SyntheticCorpusTest`, part of `./mvnw test`): every message parses, only `.example`
  domains, labels use the business schema, `inReplyTo`/`dependsOn` point backwards in time, every fact label occurs in
  the body (normalized) or is marked derivable, end state references existing beats.
- `EvalCaseLoader` reads `ai-eval/business/mailbox` unchanged (superset format); a small `BusinessMessage` record
  carries the extra fields.

### Step 2 — Per-mail extraction eval on business mail (S)
- `AiBusinessExtractionEvalTest` (`@Tag("ai-eval")`): like `AiExtractionEvalTest` with the business schema, context
  rendered from `dependsOn` via `MailContextPrompt` (so dependent mails are judged fairly), report per category and per
  tag; own baseline `ai-eval/business-baseline.json` with the one-record tolerance.

### Step 3 — Reply, courtesy and importance evals on business mail (S)
- Extend `AiReplyTriageEvalTest` / `AiReplyDraftEvalTest` to also read `ai-eval/business/mailbox` (needsReply,
  intent accuracy, question recall, scheduling windows, courtesy kinds; answered mails must get no draft).
- `AiImportanceEvalTest`: `ImportanceScorer` + signals over the labelled mails (no model) → confusion matrix of bands.

### Step 4 — Mailbox replay (L) — the new end-to-end eval
- `AiMailboxReplayEvalTest` (`@SpringBootTest` + Testcontainers + real Ollama, `@Tag("ai-eval")`): creates the owner
  with two mail accounts, inserts the mailbox in time order (in daily batches), runs the real `AiAnalysisWorker`
  (embeddings, context, extraction, proposals AUTO for records/calendar/to-dos, SUGGEST for replies) with a clock that
  follows the mailbox, runs `InsightService` at the end of each week, then compares:
  - every collection's end state with `expected/end-state.json` (precision/recall by match key, field accuracy,
    **duplicates** — e.g. the shared driver must be one person),
  - insights proposed vs `expected/insights.json` (found / late / missed, noise count),
  - reply proposals vs labels (drafts for needsReply, none for answered or CC-only, courtesy only with the setting),
  - time per email (AiTimings) and total run time.
- Runs ~1 200 mails × ~10 s ≈ 3–4 h on the RTX 3060: a `-Dai.eval.replay.limit=` option (first N days) for quick runs.

### Step 5 — Docs (S)
- README (*AI evaluation*: business dataset, how to run each eval, run times), this plan's status, ADR
  `0010-synthetic-business-dataset.md` (why fictional, labels first, different generator model).

## Acceptance criteria

- ≥ 1 150 messages, category mix within ±15 % of the table, every catalogue edge case present at least once.
- Validators green: schema, `.example` only, deny list, time order, fact check ≥ 99 % of labelled values found or
  derivable.
- Label audit agreement ≥ 90 % on records, ≥ 85 % on needsReply/intent; every disagreement resolved.
- Human review: no blocking findings in the 10 % sample.
- The four eval entry points run and print reports; the replay eval runs on a 2-week slice in < 30 min.

## Implementation notes (slice 1, 2026-10-13)

Decisions taken with the user: industrial wholesale as proposed; generated in a Claude Code session; a 4-week slice
first. Where the implementation deviates from or details the plan:

**Format and tooling**
- Tooling is dependency-free Node in `scripts/business-dataset/` (`build.mjs`, `audit.mjs`, `lib/`, tests with
  `node --test`). Phases T (merge), the validators of section 7 and E (end state) are one deterministic build.
- Storylines are stored in batch files (`storylines/<category>-<n>.json`, ≤ 5 storylines each, matching the batch rule)
  instead of one file per storyline. Phase R's output lives in the beat itself (`subject`, `body`) next to its `facts`
  and `labels`; `mailbox/` is generated from it. A beat's `key` gives stable references: `#key` inside a storyline,
  `<storyline>#<key>` across storylines (for `inReplyTo`, `dependsOn`, `answeredBy`, insight evidence).
- Numbers are declared per storyline (`numbers`) instead of a pre-allocated ledger; the validator fails on any
  order/PO/invoice/tracking-shaped number in a text that no storyline declares, and on a number declared twice. Typo
  variants (`SO-26-171`) are declared too.
- Out-of-office replies are generated from `world.absences` for every mail the owner sends to an absent person.

**Labelling conventions added**
- Sender contacts are labelled automatically: a human business sender becomes a `people` record (email; phone and role
  only when the body states them), its company an `organizations` record (domain) with `works_at`. Colleagues get a
  person only; automated senders nothing. `labels.contacts: false` turns this off (job change, cancellations).
- Sent mail carries no extraction labels: the app never extracts sent mail (`SKIPPED`), so facts only stated in a sent
  mail reach the end state only when a later inbound mail repeats them (e.g. the Vossberg order SO-26-0144 stays without
  its number). Sent mail is context (thread) and the `answeredBy` target.
- `customers`/`suppliers` records only when the mail is about the relationship (new customer, terms, leaving, price
  change); otherwise the organization is enough.
- Shipments to purchase orders use a separate relation type `delivers` (a relation type connects one pair of
  collections); `ships` is shipments → sales orders.
- End-state identity: records join on a match-key value (people also on phone), a normalized title or an explicit
  `entity` id (used for an order known first by the customer's reference, later by our SO number). `labels.retracts`
  removes a record from the end state ("sorry, wrong recipient").
- needsReply: requests to send documents or information are replies (`PROVIDE_INFO`); requests to pay, deliver or
  change something are not (they are to-dos). CC-only mails are false; a mail to the shared `orders@` mailbox counts as
  addressed to the owner.

**Label audit (phase L)** — three independent labelling passes (Claude Sonnet, separate contexts, only
`scripts/business-dataset/audit-brief.md` and the mails without labels). Agreement before / after resolving:
needsReply 96 % / 99 %, intent 78 % / 86 %, records 90 % / 91 %, importance 81 % (unchanged). Fixed: 5 needsReply
labels, 2 intents, 10 missing records. Kept: title wording differences (German vs English titles), alternative slots
the auditor turned into events, automated senders as organizations, optional courtesy replies on routine thank-yous.
Details in `ai-eval/business/audit.json`.

**Slice 1 in numbers** — 305 messages (229 inbox, 76 sent), 167 threads, 68 storylines + 63 noise mails (21 %), 66 %
German / 34 % English, 164 labelled business mails with 489 labelled records, 72 needing a reply, 5 expected insights.
Every edge case of the catalogue is tagged at least once. Smaller than the planned ≈ 400: finance (H) and meetings (F)
are thinner than the volume table; the next slice should add outgoing invoices, payment notices and win-backs.

**Evaluations** — `EvalCollections.business()`/`businessRelationTypes()`, `BusinessCorpus`/`BusinessMessage` (loader of
the superset format), `BusinessCorpusTest` (default build), `AiBusinessExtractionEvalTest`, business methods in
`AiReplyTriageEvalTest`/`AiReplyDraftEvalTest`, `AiImportanceEvalTest`, `AiMailboxReplayEvalTest`; scoring helpers
`ImportanceCase`, `QuestionMatch`, `EndStateComparison`, `InsightMatch` with unit tests (`BusinessScoringTest`).
First importance run (rules only): 76 of 163 mails in the labelled band, 12 off by two bands — mostly to-dos that a mail
marks as *done* still score HIGH (the scorer ignores `state`), and URGENT mails without the triage's urgency.
